Marta had lived beside the sea for as long as she could remember. Every morning she climbed the narrow stairs of the lighthouse, carrying a cup of strong tea for her father. The wind was usually colder than she expected, but she never complained.

One evening, a storm rolled in from the north. The waves grew taller than the garden wall, and the old lamp flickered twice before it went out. Marta’s father was away in the village, so she took a deep breath and lit the spare lantern herself.

Years later, people still talked about the night when a girl kept the light burning. Marta smiled whenever she heard the story. She knew that courage was not the absence of fear — it was simply doing what needed to be done.
