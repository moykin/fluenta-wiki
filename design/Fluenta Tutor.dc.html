Tomás had kept the light at Cabo Norte for forty years, and in all that time he had never once let it go dark. The island was small — a strip of rock, a jetty, a white tower — and he lived alone at its foot with a dog called Bruma. On the last night of October a storm came up from the south-west faster than the radio had promised. At nine o'clock the lamp's rotation gear jammed; the great beam swung once, froze, and died. Tomás climbed the hundred and four steps with a storm lantern in his hand, and from the gallery he saw it: a fishing boat, low in the water, drifting straight for the rocks.
