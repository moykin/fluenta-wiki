The Last Train

Yesterday Nina missed the last train home. She had been talking to an old friend outside the station — one of those conversations that begin with “two minutes” and quietly eat an hour. When she finally looked up, the platform was empty and the departure board had gone dark.

The next train was at six in the morning. Nina bought a coffee from the machine, sat down on the cold bench, and did the maths: seven hours, one bench, no book.

Then the friend came back. He had missed his own bus while walking her to the station, and he was carrying two paper cups and an old pack of cards. They played on the bench until the cleaners arrived, and Nina decided that some missed trains are not accidents at all.
